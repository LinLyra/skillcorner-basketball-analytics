# Literature gap matrix

This matrix prevents an unsupported “first ever” claim. It is a living review, not a completed systematic search of Scopus or Web of Science.

| Work | Data / focus | Already established | Remaining opening for this study |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daly-Grafstein & Bornn, *Using in-game shot trajectories to better understand defensive impact in the NBA* | NBA optical tracking and shot trajectories | defender distance, height, angle, and contest geometry affect shot trajectories | open-data reproduction; temporal-window comparison; explicit tracking-quality sensitivity |
| Sandholtz et al., *An Investigation of Three-point Shooting through an Analysis of NBA Player Tracking Data* | NBA SportVU | matchup distance and spatial/team context for three-point shooting | cross-game reliability and release-distance alignment audit |
| *Leveraging expected action value to measure on-court decision-making skills in basketball* | tracking-based action value | closest-defender geometry and decision value | narrow, falsifiable test of incremental temporal information rather than a new action-value framework |
| *Beyond Traditional Metrics: A Systematic Review of Spatial and Spatiotemporal Indicators in 5-on-5 Basketball* | 16-study systematic review | spatial and interaction indicators require tactical context | transparent operational definitions, failure cases, and open reproducible benchmark |
| SkillCorner Dynamic Events | professional basketball events and tracking | official shot quality, contest labels, closest defender, and closeout events | independent reconstruction and reliability analysis against raw coordinates |

## Candidate contribution statement

The defensible contribution is not “the first dynamic defensive-pressure model.” It is a reproducible evaluation of whether simple temporal defensive features add stable out-of-game predictive value beyond a release-time snapshot, together with an audit of when the underlying tracking/event alignment can be trusted.
